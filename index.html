Battery-Powered Zigbee Wireless Sensor Node

[ add a photo of the assembled board / enclosure here once cleared for external sharing ]

The problem

General Fusion's environmental sensors ran on WiFi and lasted only a few days on a full battery — too short for practical deployment in a facility running periodic plasma shots. The goal was a battery-powered replacement lasting weeks, without sacrificing sampling rate or reliability.

Hardware

I designed the sensor PCB in Altium across multiple revisions:

  • Dual temperature sensing — MAX31856 thermocouple and MAX31865 RTD channels
  • A dual-supply power system: buck-boost battery regulation, with a power-mux fallback to 5V external power
  • A 6-position rotary switch and DIP switch for field-configurable behaviour, driven off an analog input and resistor array
  • A status LED and a custom board outline shaped to fit a 3D-printed enclosure

Firmware

I wrote the low-power Zigbee end-device firmware in C using ESP-IDF, implementing sleep-mode power management that brought the device down to under 1 mA average current draw. I also configured and troubleshot the Zigbee2MQTT coordinator network the devices reported to, resolving RF and reliability issues across a multi-zone deployment.

Outcome

The sensor was deployed across the facility. I took part in the design review for the board, and later presented the project to the entire company — over 100 people.

General Fusion — 2026, Richmond, BC

Investigating an EMI-Suspected Board Failure

The problem

A fielded optical-trigger board — used to fire LED triggers for the fusion system, on a nanosecond-scale timing budget — started failing intermittently after plasma shots. Some units recovered with a power cycle; others needed the IC replaced. The root cause hadn't been confirmed, but EMI was the leading suspect given the environment.

Approach

I was asked to help investigate and harden the design in parallel:

  • Reviewed the failure pattern and identified latch-up as the leading hypothesis, based on the fact that some units recovered with just a power cycle
  • Designed, soldered, and bench-tested a layered circuit-protection revision — TVS diodes, a fuse, and ferrite filtering — explicitly balanced against the board's nanosecond-scale trigger-timing requirement so the fix wouldn't distort the signal it was protecting
  • Set up an oscilloscope-based EMI test rig to capture transients during live plasma shots, to help pin down the actual cause

Outcome

The protection revision was built and validated on the bench, and the EMI test rig is in place to keep gathering data — the investigation was still ongoing at the end of my co-op. I also took part in the design review for this board.
